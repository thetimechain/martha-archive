# 1990s Martha Stewart — Design System Reference

A research file for building a design system rooted in the original *Martha Stewart Living* visual identity (1990–1999), founded by Martha Stewart and art-directed by **Gael Towey**. The aesthetic is restrained, luminous, and craft-forward: pastoral palettes, generous white space, classical serifs, and "luminous photos and restrained typography [that] portrayed the domestic arts as anything but quaint or dowdy" *(AIGA, 2014)*.

## 3. Typography

The 90s MSL identity predated the **Archer/Surveyor** proprietary typefaces (those were commissioned from Hoefler & Frere-Jones for the **2001 redesign**, per Barbara de Wilde). In the 1990s the magazine used a mix of classical serifs and quiet sans-serifs.

### Likely fonts to use in a 90s-faithful system
- **Display / cover wordmark**: a high-contrast classical serif. *Bauer Bodoni*, *ITC Bodoni 72*, or *Didot* are the best modern stand-ins. The MSL logotype itself was a custom Bodoni-style display serif, all caps, very wide letterspacing.
- **Headlines / department titles**: *Caslon* (Adobe Caslon Pro), *Garamond Premier Pro*, or *Mrs Eaves* — quiet, classical, generous serifs.
- **Body**: a transitional serif at a generous size with airy leading. *Adobe Caslon*, *Sabon*, *Galliard*, or *Janson*.
- **Captions / metadata / labels**: a humanist sans in small caps or all caps with wide tracking. *Trade Gothic*, *Engravers Gothic*, or *Copperplate Gothic*.

### Typographic principles
- **Wide letterspacing** on display caps (the wordmark is the model).
- **Small caps** for section heads, recipe titles, and bylines.
- **Italic in titling positions** — never cute, always elegant.
- **Ragged-right body** with hyphenation, never justified blocks (unless caption columns).
- **Drop caps** in feature openings, often in a contrasting color (eggshell blue, terracotta, sage).
- **Lots of white space** — text never crowds the photograph.

## 4. Photography & art direction (the heart of the look)

This is what made the magazine. Per AIGA, the magazine pioneered "luminous photos and restrained typography."

- **Light**: natural, north-facing, soft. No hard shadows. Window light or overcast outdoor light.
- **Backgrounds**: scrubbed-pine tabletops, marble, slate, linen, weathered painted wood, plaster walls. Always tonal — the background never competes.
- **Color in subject, neutral around it**: a saturated lemon, a russet pear, a bowl of dyed eggs — placed against a near-monochrome ground.
- **Hands**: a key visual motif. Hands kneading, pouring, tying, folding. Gael Towey identified hands as "the importance of depicting hands" early in the magazine's run *(Martha Moments)*.
- **Polaroids for planning**: art directors used Polaroids extensively to scout and compose shots — that craft-driven, hands-on production shows in the final layouts.
- **Black-and-white as a deliberate choice**: e.g. James Dunlinson's all-B&W puff-pastry story — used to highlight instructional clarity and texture.
- **Crops**: generous, often square or 4:5, with the subject quietly centered or slightly off-axis. Never dramatic Dutch angles. Never busy.
- **Step-by-step photography**: a hallmark — small uniform thumbnails in a grid, each labeled with a numeral, demonstrating a process.

## 5. Layout principles

- **Generous margins**. White space is the luxury.
- **Single dominant image** per opener, often full-bleed or near-full-bleed, with a single line of display serif as the title.
- **Two-column body** with wide gutters; one-column for intimate essays.
- **Recipe and how-to integrated into the article**, not relegated to the back (a deliberate move, per Barbara de Wilde, in the later redesigns — but rooted in the 90s editorial DNA).
- **Hairline rules** (0.25–0.5 pt) in warm gray, used to separate caption blocks and recipe ingredients.
- **Numeral-led step grids** for crafts and cooking.
- **Square photo grids** for collections (eggs, pumpkins, ribbons, shells — the "taxonomy" shot).

## 6. Editorial voice (relevant for UI microcopy)

- Calm, declarative, slightly formal. "It is a good thing." "Begin with the best ingredients."
- Imperative voice in instructions, never apologetic.
- Loves the word *good*: "Good Things" was a monthly column.
- Loves *simply*, *carefully*, *gently*.
- Avoids exclamation points and superlatives.

## 7. TV show graphics & set design (1993–1999)

- The *Martha Stewart Living* television show debuted **September 1993** as a weekly half-hour, expanded to weekdays in 1997, and to a full hour in 1999 *(Wikipedia)*.
- Lower-thirds and title cards used the same classical Bodoni-style display serif as the magazine, with wide letterspacing.
- Sets were her actual Turkey Hill kitchen and gardens (Westport, CT) early on — meaning the "set design" was real architecture: white-painted plank walls, soapstone counters, copper, cream linens, baskets, glass demijohns.
- The opening titles used soft fade-ins over photographs of seasonal still lifes — flowers, eggs, ribbons — set to harpsichord/string music.

## 10. One-line system summary

> Pastoral palette + Bodoni serif + luminous natural-light photography + generous white space + the dignity of small things, well made.
